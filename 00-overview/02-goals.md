# Goals

## The main objective

I want real engineering capability out of this lab, not just familiarity with a list of tools. That means designing systems before deploying them, understanding how they fail and how to recover, applying segmentation and security on purpose rather than by default template, and documenting the work well enough that someone else could actually follow it.

## Technical skills I'm building

**Networking** — Layer 2 and Layer 3 design and enforcement, VLAN segmentation and trunking, firewall policy design and validation, switch hardening and loop prevention, and isolating and recovering from failures.

**Systems and virtualization** — deploying and hardening a hypervisor, separating VM duties properly, planning for finite resources instead of assuming I can just add more, backup and recovery strategy, and general awareness of platform lifecycle.

**Identity and trust** — internal DNS design, certificate authority concepts, service-to-service trust, and where identity boundaries actually need to sit.

**Operations and reliability** — change control discipline, phase-gated execution, rollback planning, keeping validation evidence instead of just trusting that something "looks fine," and learning from incidents the way an ops team would do a postmortem.

## Process and documentation goals

The technical skills matter, but so does how I work. I want to treat documentation as part of the system rather than something I write up after the fact. I want a repository that stays clean and reviewable, with intent, execution, and reflection kept separate instead of mashed together in one giant notes file. I want artifacts that would actually survive an audit or a handoff, and habits that would scale if I were on a team instead of working solo.

## Career goals this supports

This lab is meant to carry me from IT support toward network/systems engineering and eventually solutions architecture. By the time it's mature, it should make a pretty clear case that I'm ready for more responsibility, comfortable moving across multiple layers of infrastructure, capable of reasoning through design trade-offs, and disciplined about how I work — not just what I know.

## What I'm deliberately not doing

I'm trying to avoid collecting tools without a purpose, running undocumented experiments just to see what happens, relying on "magic" automation I don't actually understand, optimizing for speed at the cost of clarity, or treating AI (or any tooling) as a substitute for my own judgment.

## Bottom line

Every goal here points at the same outcome: become a reliable engineer who can design, implement, secure, and explain systems with confidence. Everything else is secondary to that.
