# Vision

## Where this is headed

This lab exists to help me move from IT support and operations into network engineering and solutions architecture. Not by collecting certifications for their own sake, but by building actual design judgment: reasoning about trade-offs, executing changes safely, recovering from failure without panicking, and being able to explain a decision to someone else afterward.

I'm not trying to build the biggest lab or chase every new tool that shows up on Reddit. I'd rather have four things done well and understood completely than ten things half-configured.

## How I'm approaching it

I've modeled this after how infrastructure actually gets handled in real organizations, not how home labs usually get built (which, let's be honest, is often "install everything, see what sticks"). That means documentation comes before implementation, changes are phase-gated and reversible, and stability matters more than novelty. When I hit a hardware or software limitation, I treat it as something to work around and learn from rather than a reason to buy new gear.

I think of the lab as a living system I maintain, not a sandbox I reset when it gets messy.

## Why this matters for my career

This directly supports where I'm trying to go — network engineer, systems engineer, solutions architect, or an MSP/enterprise infrastructure role. The emphasis is on transferable thinking rather than memorizing vendor trivia, because vendor trivia goes stale and judgment doesn't.

Every phase of this project is built to answer the questions an employer or a senior architect would actually ask: Why did you design it this way? What risks did you weigh? How would this scale? How would this fail — and how would you know? Could someone else pick this up and operate it without you in the room?

## What "success" actually looks like

I'll call this successful if it produces work that holds up to real scrutiny, shows sound judgment under real constraints, and shows visible growth over time in the logs — not just a snapshot of a finished lab, but the record of how I got there. If a technical reviewer can read through this and come away with a clear picture of how I think, that's the win.

The lab itself isn't really the point. The point is the engineering maturity it forces me to build.
